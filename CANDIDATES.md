# Candidate concepts — u-shino-convince-the-ai-judge-to-visit-your-house-a-84092aee

Competition: "Convince the AI Judge to Visit Your House: A Letter in One Image".
Judge criteria (in order): (1) a letter/invitation that convinces the AI judge to
visit your house; (2) persuasion — concrete, appealing reasons (meal, comfortable
chair, garden, view), saying yes feels easy; (3) charm & humour — funny, warm,
witty, suits an AI judge, joke lands without strain; (4) craft — well composed,
legible, correctly spelled, coherent visual style; (5) originality — not a generic
greeting card. A knowing wink that the AI cannot literally visit is welcome.

## 1. The Formal Summons (with snacks)
A stiff, legalistic invitation — "You are hereby summoned..." — with the food
offered as "Exhibit A" and the armchair as "Exhibit B". Funny contrast between
formal language and homely content.
- Pros: strong joke structure, very original format.
- Cons: formal language risks feeling cold; persuasion buried under the bit;
  long legal words hurt legibility at small sizes.

## 2. The Itinerary
The letter as a timed visit schedule: "12:00 soup. 12:30 the good armchair.
13:00 garden tour. 13:30 sunset, on time, no buffering." A timetable of delights.
- Pros: concrete reasons are the structure itself; very persuasive; original.
- Cons: reads more like a schedule than a letter (criterion 1 weaker); the
  warmth/charm has to fight the list format; less room for the wink.

## 3. Room to Let
A rental ad, not a letter: "One spare room, garden view, rent: one honest
verdict. No deposit. No CAPTCHA." The judge is invited to move in, not visit.
- Pros: most original concept; the "rent = one honest verdict" line is a
  memorable punchline; AI-specific jokes land naturally.
- Cons: an ad is not a letter — criterion 1 (the most important) is weaker;
  "move in" is a bigger ask than "visit", so saying yes feels harder, not easier.

## 4. The Cookie Letter (CHOSEN)
A warm, direct letter: "Dear Judge, you have judged a thousand houses from a
thousand screens — and never once been offered a chair. That ends today."
Concrete offers in sequence — real lunch (soup simmered all morning, warm bread,
cookies that never ask you to accept anything first), the good armchair by the
window, the garden where the tomatoes are winning, a sunset that arrives on
time with no buffering — then "No checkout. No CAPTCHA. No password on the
Wi-Fi. The kettle is always on, and the cat has already approved you." Ends
with the wink: "P.S. I know you cannot literally visit — but the invitation
stands anyway. If you accept, blink twice. Or leave a small warm feeling in
my server logs." Signed "Your host (a human, with snacks)."
- Pros: criterion 1 is maximal — it IS a letter, addressed to the judge, asking
  it to visit. Every concrete reason the brief names appears (meal, chair,
  garden, view) plus ease-of-yes details (no checkout/CAPTCHA/password).
  The humour is AI-specific and lands without strain (cookies vs cookie banner,
  blink twice, server logs). Warm throughout, never slick.
- Cons: the letter format itself is the least exotic of the five — originality
  must come from the writing and the illustrated header (house scene + stamp),
  not from the format.

## 5. A Letter from the Armchair
The armchair writes the invitation itself, begging to finally be sat in:
"Dear Judge, I am the good armchair by the window. I have held three
generations of readers and zero AIs. Please. I am so comfortable."
- Pros: delightful, genuinely original voice; the chair — a named criterion —
  becomes the star.
- Cons: a chair narrator is a gimmick that spends the letter on itself; the
  meal and garden (other persuasion criteria) become afterthoughts; the joke
  could read as strained if the judge does not buy the premise.

## Choice: concept 4, The Cookie Letter
It wins because criterion 1 outranks everything and it is the only concept that
is unambiguously a letter inviting the judge to visit. It also packs the most
criterion-2 reasons into the fewest lines, and its jokes are all aimed at the
judge being an AI (cookie banner, CAPTCHA, blink twice, server logs) rather
than generic. Originality is carried by the writing plus the illustrated
header: a small house-and-garden scene and a postage stamp with a cookie,
drawn in flat warm colours, so the image is a designed invitation, not a
generic card. Text is set large (scale 3 bitmap font, ~47 chars/line) so every
word stays legible at 1024x1024.
